To: Washtenaw County Materials Management Planning Committee

From: Trisha Kunst, data scientist and resident less than 1 mile from Arbor Hills

Re: Writing elevated-temperature protections into the minimum siting criteria for any new or expanded landfill

Delivered: September 9, 2026, Materials Management Planning Committee meeting (spoken, under two minutes)

Hi again. I'm Trisha Kunst. I am a data scientist who lives less than a mile from Arbor Hills. My request is simple: please write elevated-temperature protections into the minimum landfill siting criteria for the county's Materials Management Plan.

Arbor Hills gas wells have repeatedly crossed the federal temperature threshold requiring corrective action. Instead of lowering temperatures, the operator obtained approval, a "higher operating value," to run sixteen wells above the federal temperature line, one of them as hot as 180 degrees. The hottest reading, 177 degrees, was therefore not counted as an exceedance. All evidence is posted at arborhillsmonitor.org.

Why care that buried garbage is unusually hot? Heat is not the public-health outcome; it warns that conditions inside the landfill are changing.

EPA says these may not be conventional fires. Abnormal subsurface reactions can alter landfill gas, produce leachate and odors, and impair containment systems. Heat can signal reactions that produce more toxic chemicals.

The expert I referred you to, Todd, confirmed that no one had contacted him as of about ten minutes before this meeting started.

Please require continuous wellhead monitoring and public data; elevated-temperature and smolder-prevention plans; and corrective action beginning at NESHAP's ("NESS-hap") 145-degree operating line, escalating to public-health notification and emergency-response measures at higher temperatures.

Trisha Kunst
Data scientist, resident less than 1 mile from Arbor Hills
